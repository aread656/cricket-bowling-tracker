# ISSUE1 - Isolating Trajectories (Splitting Video)
The trajectories for each delivery shall be isolated from all others, and this may be done through splitting the video into individual deliveries. There are some theorised solutions for this:
    1. Split the video when the ball is not detected across a certain threshold of frames. If this threshold is met, then the delivery is deemed to be over and a new video is created.
        Pros: Works when raw video is fed to the system
        Cons: Will keep in footage of the user picking up the ball, as the ball often bounces back and stops in view of the camera. Will also keep in footage of the ball continuing to bounce after would be deemed to be a dead ball (after it has cleared its necessary distance, the wicket/wall).
    2. Analyse the trajectory of the delivery. Fit a curve to the trajectory and find the second minima of the curve through second-order differentiation. Once a second minima has been reached, end the footage for the delivery
        Pros: Cuts footage as soon as the ball is dead. Could be improved by making an ML model trained on dead and non-dead deliveries, and cut the footage when the ball reaches the wall or wicket. No additional footage of ball pickup or extra bouncing after deemed dead.
        Cons: Trajectory will still record the ball bouncing back, e.g. if a ball hits the wicket and bounces off, then hits the ground again. This method will include within the trajectory the segment of the delivery where the ball bounces back. This is also an issue if using method 1. May also require greater code complexity.
        This is a separate issue, but this method would be more useful were the system able to detect when the ball hits the wall or wicket. It is, however, an improvement on method 1.
    3. Create a new trajectory when there is a large jump in trajectory. Make a threshold for how big a jump can be made between the last recorded position and the current position, and create a new trajectory if this threshold is surpassed
        Pros: May be quite simple to create. Only some small testing for parameter tuning needed. Good for video footage where the dead space between deliveries has been removed, but the deliveries themselves have not been segmented into individual footage instances.
        Cons: Suffers from similar problems to those of method 1, except includes all dead space since the video itself is not segmented, just the trajectories. Could be solved by using a combined approach, however.
